Taco & Pisco Night

Two tacos that cook themselves while you're out haunting the shops, a pisco sour bar run by your guests, and about 15 minutes of actual cooking once the doorbell goes.

The two fillings

Everything else waits on the toppings table: pico de gallo, avocado crema, purple slaw, pickled chillies, coriander and lime. Guests build their own.

Recipes

Oven · 3 h unattended

Oven carnitas

  • 1.2 kg pork shoulder, 5 cm chunks
  • 120 ml orange juice
  • 4 bay leaves
  • 2 tsp each sea salt, ground cumin
  • 1 tsp each chilli powder, garlic powder, onion powder, dried oregano, black pepper
  1. Heat oven to 165°C. Toss pork with the spices in a casserole dish.
  2. Add the juice and bay, plus water to come about halfway up the meat. Lid on.
  3. Cook about 3 h until it pulls apart. Shred.
  4. To serve, fry in a hot pan until the edges crisp.

Adapted from Grantourismo (spices, quantities, 165°C, 3 h; serves 8) and Edible Phoenix (covered, half-submerged method).

Hob · 15 min · make at 18:00

Chicken tinga

  • 900 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs, in 1 cm pieces
  • 2 large white onions, sliced
  • 6 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 2 × 400 g tins diced tomatoes, fire-roasted if you can, lightly drained
  • 6–8 chipotles in adobo, thinly sliced
  • 2 tsp dried oregano (Mexican if possible)
  • Oil; about 2 tsp salt; a splash of vinegar
  1. Large frying pan, medium-high. Fry the onion about 4 min until browned.
  2. Add chicken in one layer, brown 5–6 min. Add garlic, 1 more min.
  3. Roughly blitz the tomatoes and add with the chipotles and oregano.
  4. Lower the heat, simmer about 4 min until thick and tender. Season with salt and vinegar.
  5. Lid on, set aside. Reheat gently at 19:45.

Doubled from a 12-taco recipe. Start with 6 chipotles if anyone is wary of heat.

Rick Bayless, chicken tinga tacos (canned-chipotle option), doubled. Making it ahead and reheating is my suggestion; the recipe doesn't cover it.

Toppings table · all prepped before 19:00

Pico, slaw, crema, pickled chillies

  • Pico de gallo: dice 4 plum tomatoes, 1 cup white onion, 4 serranos, ⅔ cup coriander. Add juice of 2 limes and 1 tsp salt.
  • Purple slaw: finely shred 1 small red cabbage, toss with 1 tsp sea salt and juice of 2 limes. Rest 20 min, tossing now and then.
  • Avocado crema: blend 2 avocados, 6 tbsp crème fraîche, a handful of coriander and juice of 1–2 limes. Loosen with water until it drizzles.
  • Pickled chillies (day before): simmer 100 ml white wine vinegar, 50 g caster sugar, 100 ml water and 4 sliced red chillies for 2 min, then cool.
  • Plus radishes, feta or queso fresco, coriander leaves and lime wedges.

Pico from Mely Martínez, doubled. Slaw, crema and pickled chillies from Olive magazine, crema scaled 1.5×.

Bar · guests make it

Pisco sour, blender batch for 6

  • 360 ml Peruvian pisco (Quebranta)
  • 120 ml fresh lime juice
  • 120 ml sugar syrup (1:1 sugar and water)
  • 1 egg white
  • A handful of ice
  • Angostura bitters
  1. Blend everything except the bitters for 1 minute, until thick and frothy.
  2. Half-fill six glasses, then top up each so the foam is shared evenly.
  3. Drop 1–3 drops of bitters on the foam. Don't stir. (Three drops look like eyes. You're welcome.)

Shaker version, one drink: 60 ml pisco, 20 ml lime, 20 ml syrup, ⅓ egg white. Shake hard without ice, then again with ice, strain into a chilled coupe.

How much pisco: two rounds of six at 60 ml each is 720 ml, so one 70 cl bottle is just short. Buy two.

Story for the table: the drink is usually credited to Victor Morris's bar in Lima, around 1916. A bartender who worked for him, Mario Bruiget, is said to have added the egg white and bitters. Chile disputes all of it.

Batch from The Pisco People (240/80/80 ml for 4, scaled 1.5×); dry-then-wet shake from Tasting Table and Aglaia; history from MasterClass and Punch.
